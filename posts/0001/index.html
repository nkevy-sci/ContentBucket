Variability & Golf

credit: Hooke, 1983 as cited by Stark, 2023.

The following excellent example of variability comes from P.B. Stark and Robert Hooke.

Two golfers’ scores are described in the following table.

Jill8177777778

Frequency

First, recognize that the golfer named Jack scored 78 in every game.

Can you express Jack’s performance as a percentage?

This percentage is called a frequency. In other words, how frequently did Jack score 78? Every time, 100% of the time. There was no variabilityat all. But what about Jill?

Can you express Jill’s performance as a percentage?

Jill’s scores
GolferScore 1Frequency 1Score 2Frequency 2
Jill81—25%—77—75%—

So Jill scored an 81 in 25% of her games.

Well now, in Game 1 who won Jack or Jill?

Remember in golf, whoever has the lower score wins.

So Jack won Game 1.

Prediction

Suppose that Jack and Jill always have the same performance. In the next game, Jill scores 77 and Jack scores 78.

What were the odds of this happening? Does it help us to know their averages?

No, knowing their average score doesn’t help us much. The interesting thing is that Jack and Jill both have the same average score. Yet most of the time Jill will beat Jack.
